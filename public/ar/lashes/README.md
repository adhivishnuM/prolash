# PaoLash procedural eyelash assets — prototype v1.3

These are original, editable procedural strand meshes generated by `lib/ar/lash-geometry.ts`. They are not hand-groomed Blender assets, measurements of a salon treatment, or artist-approved production models. No third-party face mesh, photograph, texture, or purchased lash model is included.

Six grooms use individual length maps, curl, spread and direction. Each exported eye is one merged mesh of individually curled, tapered, four-sided fibres. The browser generates these meshes locally from the same editable source; the portable GLBs are exported reference deliverables, not extra camera-session downloads.

The live renderer uses the deformed 3D strand centrelines to build camera-facing fibre ribbons with feathered, coverage-corrected edges. This avoids the dotted appearance of subpixel tube triangles. The strands still follow the three-dimensional eyelid and head pose; this is not a fixed image of a lash strip. There are no texture downloads or visible strip bands. Every style uses two strand draws plus one invisible face-depth draw. A capped ~2 MP backing buffer provides extra antialiasing samples, automatically reduced to ~1 MP on slower devices. Near-black unlit fibre colour avoids artificial silver specular dashes.

The v1.3 grooms separate their profiles through explicit length maps, fan widths and curl settings. Wet fibres converge to cluster tips; Wispy has longer accents above its shorter underlayer; Doll concentrates its height over the centre; Eyeliner concentrates length and outward direction at the outer corner. The live definition control changes strand opacity through an optical-density curve; it leaves the style's length, curl and attachment geometry intact. The default is 65%, with room to soften or intensify the preview.

Run `node scripts/generate-lash-assets.mjs` to regenerate all GLBs, matching selector SVGs and the manifest. SVGs project the actual neutral grooms at the same scale, preserving visible length/density differences. Run with `--check` to test geometry and verify the checked-in assets still match the source. The script uses the project-pinned Three.js 0.186.1 exporter and loader, validates both eyes' triangle winding, normals, finite positions, attachment interpolation, blink length preservation, deterministic regeneration, mirrored orientation, triangle/size budgets, preview bounds and a GLB export-and-reload. A runtime pass on real devices remains separate from these checks.

## Coordinates and deformation

The portable meshes use Y up, Z out of the face and one arbitrary unit per neutral eye width; this is not a millimetre estimate. Eye labels are anatomical. In the unmirrored source camera, the wearer's left eye appears on image-right. Selfie mirroring is applied to the complete camera composite by the UI, never to the landmark data.

Every mesh's glTF `extras.fibers` records a root parameter (0 inner to 1 outer), vertex start and vertex count. The procedural source retains strand-local positions and normals. Runtime deformation samples the nine-point upper-lid curve, forms an orthonormal tangent/up/forward basis, and rotates the profile through 0–1.4 radians without shortening it. Closure is calibrated from the actual 3D eyelid aperture, with the blendshape as supporting evidence; an open eye's raw blink score no longer directly folds its lashes down. Static GLBs do not automatically perform this fitting; the runtime implementation is `lib/ar/lash-renderer.ts` plus the shared source. These exports contain the neutral rest pose, not a skinned rig or blink morph targets.

The runtime scales MediaPipe depth with video width, converts image-down into scene-up, fits the eyes independently and applies brief time-aware landmark smoothing. It hides tiny or very oblique fitted eyes. The camera remains the background; it is never part of the 3D asset.

## Remaining visual approval work

The grooms' curl, density and length distributions are initial art direction, not confirmed PaoLash treatment specifications. Obtain the lash artist's reference measurements and approval, calibrate root placement and blink rotation on consenting participants, review different eye shapes and skin tones, and test real iPhone/Android cameras before a public launch. No real-camera realism or performance acceptance targets have been claimed as passed.

Current rendering includes a tracked face-depth mesh with eye openings, so nose and cheeks can hide far-side fibres. It has no hand/hair segmentation, environmental lighting estimation, or removal of existing lashes/mascara. Existing heavy extensions, glasses, extreme pose and difficult lighting can produce an inaccurate additive preview. Only the standard geometry tier is currently authored. A Blender master, artist-approved boards, alternative quality tiers and recorded real-device proof captures remain future asset-production work.
